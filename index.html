A shared evidence language

Uncertainty about the explanation does not erase the event.

You are allowed to document violence, deception, coercion, stalking, contradictory records, financial harm, identity misuse, or other events even when you cannot yet explain why. A strong pattern deserves investigation. A hypothesis remains a hypothesis until evidence supports it.

01

Documented / verified

Supported by an original record, independent witness, court finding, official report, or reproducible technical evidence.

02

Research-supported

A mechanism recognized in credible research, law, guidance, or established case literature.

03

Survivor allegation

A person’s account, retained as an allegation unless and until separate evidence corroborates it.

04

Inference / hypothesis

A proposed link, motive, actor, or explanation that can be tested against evidence and alternatives.

05

Unknown / contradicted

Not enough reliable information, or credible evidence conflicts with the claim. Preserve the conflict rather than smoothing it over.

What the evidence recognizes

The mechanisms recur. The explanation must still be earned.

Courts, regulators, researchers, and public agencies document overlapping tactics across otherwise different settings. Similar tactics do not by themselves prove a shared actor, motive, or worldwide system.

Course of conduct

Repeated control

Isolation, monitoring, humiliation, threats, deprivation, and rule changes may only become clear when assessed cumulatively.

Technology

Access turned into control

Account access, location sharing, stalkerware, smart devices, impersonation, doxxing, and non-consensual images can extend abuse beyond physical proximity.

Narrative

Credibility displacement

False or selective information, malicious reports, shifting standards, and claims that a victim is unstable can redirect attention from verifiable conduct.

Institutions

Systems used as leverage

Legal process, police reports, medical systems, immigration status, benefits, or child-contact proceedings can be threatened or misused in documented cases.

Economic

Identity and financial control

Debt, account takeover, forged applications, withheld documents, employment interference, or control of money can restrict a person’s choices.

Recruitment

Trust, collateral, and coercion

Documented criminal cases show trust-building, romantic or mentoring roles, compromising material, threats, and forced labor used together.

Survivor-described marker

Persistent narrative commitment

Some survivors describe the same calm false frame being maintained from early contact through severe consequences, with no obvious “reveal.” MAXLIGHTING records this as a testable description. It is not yet an established clinical or legal term, and persistence alone does not identify intent or coordination.

Before collecting digital evidence

Use a safer device if possible. Do not access someone else’s account, impersonate anyone, or break the law to obtain evidence. Preserve originals and seek local legal advice about recording-consent rules. If removing a tracker or changing an account could trigger escalation, speak with a specialist first.

A connected pattern of tactics. Not evidence of one global actor.

The evidence supports comparison across coercive control, cyberstalking, institutional failure, forced labor, identity abuse, and organized online coercion. It also requires hard boundaries around what remains unknown.

Read the full methodology and source ledger

Supported

What current evidence can show

  • Nonphysical coercion can produce serious, cumulative harm.
  • Technology can extend monitoring, impersonation, threats, and control.
  • Trusted roles and institutions can be used to gain access or displace a victim’s account.
  • Authorities have sometimes dismissed credible reports or created inaccurate records before later scrutiny.
  • Disabled and otherwise vulnerable groups can face elevated victimization and added barriers to help.

Not established

What the evidence does not show

  • That one organization causes all similar experiences worldwide.
  • That intensity, persistence, or similarity alone identifies intent or a perpetrator.
  • That a checklist can determine whether someone is “targeted.”
  • That neurodivergent people are selected by a single coordinated network.
  • That every disputed record is false or every official response is corrupt.

Documented / verifiedUK · Court case

F v M [2021] EWFC 4

Establishes: On the civil standard in that family case, the court assessed alleged coercive control cumulatively and examined isolation, communications, shifting accounts, and allegations rather than treating each event alone.

Limit: A family-court fact finding is not a criminal conviction and does not establish a wider network.

Documented / verifiedUK · Court case

R v Challen [2019] EWCA Crim 916

Establishes: Fresh psychiatric evidence, considered in the context of an allegedly controlling relationship, undermined the safety of the murder conviction and led to it being quashed.

Limit: The appeal court expressly did not decide whether coercive control occurred; coercive control alone was not the legal basis for the result.

Establishes: The Metropolitan Police admitted that some undercover officers entered long-term intimate relationships that were abusive, deceitful, manipulative, and an abuse of police power; a tribunal later found multiple rights violations in Wilson’s case.

A bounded research question

Vulnerability deserves protection—not a preloaded theory.

Official evidence shows that disabled people can experience higher rates of victimization and face additional barriers to recognition and support. Social isolation, dependence on a caregiver, communication barriers, and economic disadvantage can increase exposure or reduce safe options.

That supports better accessibility, safeguarding, and data collection. It does not by itself prove organized selection of autistic, ADHD, adopted, immigrant, disabled, or otherwise marginalized people.

Life insurance and identity misuse

There is no universal public registry of policies on a living person.

A negative result from one service cannot prove that no application, policy, or attempted fraud exists. Use the route that matches what you are trying to verify.

If you are alive and concerned about an application

Request your MIB Consumer File

If you applied for individually underwritten life or health insurance with an MIB member company within the last seven years, a file may exist. It can show some coded underwriting information and recent inquiries. It is not a list of every policy.

Request the official file (opens in new tab)
If you suspect forgery, agent misconduct, or a fake policy

Contact the state insurance regulator

Give the regulator the insurer or agent name if known, dates, documents, application details, and the specific reason you suspect fraud. Ask what records the regulator can obtain.

Find the official regulator (opens in new tab)
If the insured person has died

Use the NAIC Policy Locator

The locator is for beneficiaries or authorized representatives looking for a deceased person’s life-insurance policy or annuity. NAIC says searches may take 90 business days or more.

Open the NAIC locator (opens in new tab)
If identity information may be compromised

Build an identity-theft recovery plan

Close or secure affected accounts, review credit reports, consider fraud alerts or freezes, and make a tailored recovery plan. A credit freeze does not stop every insurance-purpose inquiry or takeover of an existing account.

Use IdentityTheft.gov (opens in new tab)
Insurable interest matters.

NAIC consumer guidance says a person buying life insurance on someone else must have an insurable interest, and a stranger cannot lawfully buy a policy simply to profit from the person’s death. Exact rules and remedies vary by state.
