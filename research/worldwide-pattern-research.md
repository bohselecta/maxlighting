# MAXLIGHTING worldwide pattern research

**Research snapshot:** September 3, 2026
**Scope:** coercive control, persistent false narratives, technology-facilitated abuse, stalking, impersonation, identity and financial exploitation, forced labor, organized online coercion, institutional failure, and barriers affecting vulnerable people.
**Public-safety posture:** no private names, no public accusation matching, and no assumption that similar cases share an actor.

## Executive conclusion

The research supports a strong, defensible conclusion: **the component mechanisms are real, documented across jurisdictions, and capable of severe cumulative harm**. Courts and agencies recognize repeated coercion, technology-enabled monitoring, false or malicious information, misuse of process, economic control, trust-based recruitment, blackmail, forced labor, and institutional failures to act.

The research does **not** establish that one organization, ideology, country, or criminal system causes every similar experience. The same tactic can appear in intimate-partner abuse, cultic or group control, trafficking, online networks, fraud, workplace or institutional misconduct, and ordinary criminal offending. Similarity is a lead for careful comparison, not proof of coordination.

## Finding labels

Every public claim should use one of these labels:

1. **DOCUMENTED / VERIFIED** — directly supported by an original record, court finding, official report, or reproducible technical evidence.
2. **RESEARCH-SUPPORTED** — a pattern supported by credible research, law, or official guidance but not necessarily proved in a particular report.
3. **SURVIVOR ALLEGATION** — a person’s account that has not been independently established.
4. **INFERENCE / HYPOTHESIS** — a proposed connection, motive, actor, or explanation that remains testable.
5. **UNKNOWN / CONTRADICTED** — evidence is insufficient, disputed, or conflicts with a stronger source.

These labels are not a hierarchy of human worth. They are a way to keep a record usable.

## High-confidence findings

- **DOCUMENTED / VERIFIED:** Coercive control can consist of repeated or continuous behavior whose significance is visible only when incidents are assessed together. England and Wales statutory guidance includes emotional, technological, and economic abuse; stalking; false or malicious information; hacking; fake accounts; doxxing; surveillance; and misuse of legal or administrative processes. [UK Home Office statutory guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/controlling-or-coercive-behaviour-statutory-guidance-framework/controlling-or-coercive-behaviour-statutory-guidance-framework-accessible)
- **DOCUMENTED / VERIFIED:** Courts have accepted records in which a perpetrator monitored location, appeared repeatedly, denied corroborated events, or called the victim “crazy” as part of a larger stalking or harassment course of conduct. [O.D. v. J.S., A-3904-23 (N.J. App. Div. 2026)](https://law.justia.com/cases/new-jersey/appellate-division-unpublished/2026/a-3904-23.html)
- **DOCUMENTED / VERIFIED:** Technology-facilitated abuse can include account access, location tracking, hidden monitoring, impersonation, non-consensual imagery, threats, and control through connected devices. [Australian eSafety guidance](https://www.esafety.gov.au/key-topics/tech-based-abuse/i-may-be-experiencing-it), [Council of Europe overview](https://www.coe.int/en/web/cdpc/technology-facilitated-violence-against-women-and-girls)
- **DOCUMENTED / VERIFIED:** Criminal cases show trust, status, intimate or mentoring roles, compromising “collateral,” false confessions, threats, financial dependence, and psychological abuse being used with forced labor or sexual exploitation. [DOJ summary of United States v. Raniere](https://www.justice.gov/usao-edny/pr/nxivm-leader-keith-raniere-sentenced-120-years-prison-racketeering-and-sex-trafficking), [DOJ summary of United States v. Lawrence Ray](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/lawrence-ray-convicted-racketeering-violent-assault-extortion-sex-trafficking-forced)
- **DOCUMENTED / VERIFIED:** Institutions sometimes fail to act on credible reports, create inaccurate or incomplete records, or give excessive weight to flawed system evidence. Later official inquiries have documented specific failures in the Nassar, Rotherham, and Post Office Horizon matters. [DOJ OIG Nassar review](https://oig.justice.gov/reports/investigation-and-review-federal-bureau-investigations-handling-allegations-sexual-abuse), [Rotherham independent inquiry](https://www.rotherham.gov.uk/downloads/file/279/independent-inquiry-into-child-sexual-exploitationin-rotherham), [Post Office Horizon Inquiry reports](https://www.postofficehorizoninquiry.org.uk/reports-and-statements)
- **RESEARCH-SUPPORTED:** Disabled people can face elevated victimization and additional barriers to recognition, reporting, and support. This justifies accessible safeguarding and better data; it does not prove organized selection of autistic or otherwise neurodivergent people. [Australia Disability Royal Commission, Volume 3](https://disability.royalcommission.gov.au/publications/final-report-volume-3-nature-and-extent-violence-abuse-neglect-and-exploitation), [ONS victim-characteristics data](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/crimeandjustice/articles/domesticabusevictimcharacteristicsenglandandwales/latest), [U.S. BJS disability victimization tables, 2009–2019](https://bjs.ojp.gov/library/publications/crime-against-persons-disabilities-2009-2019-statistical-tables)
- **UNKNOWN:** “Persistent narrative commitment,” including the survivor phrase “100% commitment to the role,” is not an established clinical or legal construct in the reviewed literature. It can be retained as a survivor-described, testable pattern: did the same claim persist, what contemporaneous evidence contradicted or supported it, and what alternative explanations fit?

## Method

### Source hierarchy

The review prioritized:

1. court judgments and official case records;
2. inspector-general, coronial, parliamentary, royal-commission, and public-inquiry reports;
3. statutes and official government guidance;
4. UN, Council of Europe, and national regulator material;
5. peer-reviewed or official statistical research.

News, survivor narratives, social posts, and blogs can generate leads but were not used to establish public factual claims in this report without an independent primary source.

### Comparison method

Each case was checked for a defined set of mechanisms: cumulative control, isolation, threats, violence, sexual coercion, financial control, identity or document misuse, technology surveillance, impersonation, malicious allegations, process misuse, reputational harm, group coercion, official misconduct, initial disbelief, later corroboration, disability or other vulnerability, cross-border conduct, online recruitment, blackmail, and forced labor.

The comparison deliberately separates:

- **pattern alignment** — resemblance to a recognized mechanism;
- **evidence strength** — quality and provenance of material supporting a specific event; and
- **cross-case linkage** — uncommon objective identifiers shared by independently developed records.

No combined “probability of being targeted” score is scientifically justified by this evidence.

### Limitations

- This is a rigorous public-source review, not a systematic review of every country or language.
- English-accessible primary sources are overrepresented.
- Court findings use different standards of proof and cannot be combined as if they were equivalent.
- A criminal conviction, civil finding, official allegation, threat assessment, and prevalence study answer different questions.
- Published cases overrepresent severe and institutionally visible events.
- Similar terminology can hide material differences in local law.
- Contact routes and statutes change; all help links require periodic review.

## Twelve strongest case, inquiry, and enforcement matches

### 1. F v M [2021] EWFC 4 — England and Wales

**Status:** DOCUMENTED / VERIFIED court findings on the civil standard.
**Documented mechanisms:** isolation, communications restrictions, competing narratives, allegations, use of police, and the need to assess behavior cumulatively. The judgment criticized an earlier acceptance of one account without sufficient “forensic curiosity.”
**Why it matters:** It demonstrates how a sequence can be misunderstood when each event is isolated from the course of conduct.
**Material difference:** This was family-court fact finding, not a criminal conviction, and it does not establish coordination beyond the parties.
**Source:** [BAILII judgment](https://www.bailii.org/ew/cases/EWFC/HCJ/2021/4.html)

### 2. R v Challen [2019] EWCA Crim 916 — England and Wales

**Status:** DOCUMENTED / VERIFIED appellate ruling.
**Documented mechanisms:** an allegedly controlling relationship, isolation and intimidation evidence, missed psychiatric conditions, and a prior framing of the appellant as obsessively jealous.
**Outcome:** The murder conviction was quashed because fresh psychiatric evidence undermined its safety.
**Material difference:** The court expressly did not decide whether coercive control occurred, and stated that coercive control by itself was not a defense to murder.
**Source:** [BAILII judgment](https://www.bailii.org/ew/cases/EWCA/Crim/2019/916.html)

### 3. O.D. v J.S., A-3904-23 (2026) — New Jersey, United States

**Status:** DOCUMENTED / VERIFIED unpublished appellate opinion; binding only on the parties and not precedential.
**Documented mechanisms:** tracking through a child’s device, repeated appearances, remote alarm activation, denial of events supported by video or admissions, and calling the victim “crazy.”
**Outcome:** The appellate court affirmed a final restraining order based on stalking and harassment and deferred to detailed credibility findings supported by the record.
**Material difference:** It is a domestic-violence protective-order case with a preponderance standard, not a criminal conviction.
**Source:** [Opinion mirror with publication limitation](https://law.justia.com/cases/new-jersey/appellate-division-unpublished/2026/a-3904-23.html)

### 4. Volodina v Russia (no. 2), application 40419/19 — European Court of Human Rights

**Status:** DOCUMENTED / VERIFIED human-rights judgment.
**Documented mechanisms:** fake profiles, publication of intimate images, location tracking, social-media threats, and inadequate investigation.
**Outcome:** The court unanimously found that the authorities had failed to protect the applicant from repeated cyberviolence and had not carried out an effective investigation.
**Material difference:** The judgment determines one state’s human-rights responsibility in one record; it does not imply cross-case coordination.
**Source:** [ECHR judgment summary](https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/app/conversion/pdf/?filename=Judgment+Volodina+v.+Russia+&id=003-7116031-9638379&library=ECHR)

### 5. Kate Wilson v Metropolitan Police — United Kingdom

**Status:** DOCUMENTED / VERIFIED admission and tribunal findings.
**Documented mechanisms:** a long-term intimate relationship created by an undercover officer using a false identity, surveillance, deception, and interference with protected rights.
**Outcome:** The Metropolitan Police apologized for officers’ long-term intimate relationships and described them as abusive, deceitful, manipulative, wrong, and an abuse of police power; the Investigatory Powers Tribunal found multiple violations in Wilson’s case.
**Material difference:** This is bounded, proven official misconduct. It is not evidence that a government role should be inferred in unrelated cases.
**Sources:** [Investigatory Powers Tribunal decision](https://www.judiciary.uk/judgments/wilson-v-mps/), [Metropolitan Police background and apology](https://www.met.police.uk/SysSiteAssets/media/downloads/force-content/met/about-us/undercover-policing-inquiry/Undercover-Policing-Inquiry-further-information.pdf)

### 6. Inquest into the deaths of Hannah Clarke, her children, and Rowan Baxter — Queensland, Australia

**Status:** DOCUMENTED / VERIFIED coronial findings.
**Documented mechanisms:** sustained coercive control, isolation, jealousy, threats, post-separation escalation, and interactions with police and services.
**Why it matters:** The inquest places separate behaviors into a risk pattern and identifies opportunities for system learning.
**Material difference:** Risk factors do not make outcomes inevitable, and this fatal case should not be used to predict another person’s conduct without an individual risk assessment.
**Source:** [Queensland Coroners Court findings](https://www.courts.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0010/723664/cif-hannah-clarke-aaliyah-baxter-laianah-baxter-trey-baxter-and-rowan-baxter.pdf)

### 7. United States v Keith Raniere — New York, United States

**Status:** DOCUMENTED / VERIFIED criminal convictions.
**Documented mechanisms:** false recruitment into a supposed women-only mentorship group, hierarchy and obedience, damaging confessions and intimate material demanded as “collateral,” threats of exposure, forced labor, and sexual exploitation.
**Outcome:** A federal jury convicted Raniere of racketeering, sex-trafficking, forced-labor, and wire-fraud offenses; he received a 120-year sentence.
**Material difference:** The evidence established one criminal enterprise. It cannot be used to label unrelated organizations or relationships.
**Source:** [U.S. Attorney’s Office case summary](https://www.justice.gov/usao-edny/pr/nxivm-leader-keith-raniere-sentenced-120-years-prison-racketeering-and-sex-trafficking)

### 8. United States v Lawrence Ray — New York, United States

**Status:** DOCUMENTED / VERIFIED criminal convictions.
**Documented mechanisms:** trust and authority within a student social group, psychological and physical abuse, threats, extortion, coerced labor, financial exploitation, and sex trafficking.
**Outcome:** A federal jury convicted Ray on every count presented.
**Material difference:** The convictions concern Ray and the charged enterprise; they do not supply a general test for detecting a hidden group.
**Source:** [U.S. Attorney’s Office conviction summary](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/lawrence-ray-convicted-racketeering-violent-assault-extortion-sex-trafficking-forced)

### 9. DOJ OIG review of FBI handling of Larry Nassar allegations — United States

**Status:** DOCUMENTED / VERIFIED inspector-general findings.
**Documented mechanisms:** failure to respond with urgency, inaccurate or incomplete documentation, false statements by officials, and abuse continuing during delays.
**Why it matters:** It is a primary-source example in which serious reports were mishandled and the official record itself was unreliable in important respects.
**Material difference:** The report documents specific personnel and process failures. It does not establish that a disappointing or adverse FBI response in another matter is deliberate misconduct.
**Source:** [DOJ OIG investigation and review](https://oig.justice.gov/reports/investigation-and-review-federal-bureau-investigations-handling-allegations-sexual-abuse)

### 10. Post Office Horizon IT Inquiry — United Kingdom

**Status:** DOCUMENTED / VERIFIED public-inquiry record.
**Documented mechanisms:** flawed computer evidence, institutional resistance to contrary accounts, wrongful accusations and prosecutions, and extensive human consequences.
**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that apparently authoritative records can be wrong and that institutions can sustain those records despite repeated contradiction.
**Material difference:** This is a software, governance, disclosure, and prosecution failure—not evidence of interpersonal gaslighting or one actor managing victims’ lives.
**Source:** [Post Office Horizon IT Inquiry reports and statements](https://www.postofficehorizoninquiry.org.uk/reports-and-statements)

### 11. Rotherham independent inquiry into child sexual exploitation — United Kingdom

**Status:** DOCUMENTED / VERIFIED independent inquiry.
**Documented mechanisms:** organized sexual exploitation, threats and violence, victim vulnerability, reports not acted upon, and institutional cultures that minimized or ignored evidence.
**Finding:** The inquiry estimated that at least 1,400 children were sexually exploited in Rotherham from 1997 to 2013.
**Material difference:** The estimate and mechanisms concern a defined place, population, and period. They do not imply a universal model.
**Source:** [Alexis Jay independent inquiry report](https://www.rotherham.gov.uk/downloads/file/279/independent-inquiry-into-child-sexual-exploitationin-rotherham)

### 12. FTC action against SpyFone — United States

**Status:** DOCUMENTED regulatory enforcement; complaint allegations were resolved by consent order rather than fully adjudicated at trial.
**Documented action:** The FTC imposed a surveillance-business ban, data-deletion requirements, and device-owner notification requirements. The complaint alleged hidden monitoring of locations, messages, photos, calls, and online activity and inadequate security.
**Why it matters:** It shows that consumer surveillance products can enable covert intimate monitoring and create additional data risk.
**Material difference:** Complaint allegations should remain labeled as allegations; the order does not prove installation on any individual reporter’s device.
**Source:** [FTC SpyFone case record and order](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/support-king-llc-spyfonecom-matter-timeline-item-2021-08-26)

## Official mechanism evidence outside the case matrix

### Coercive control and process misuse

The [UK Home Office statutory guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/controlling-or-coercive-behaviour-statutory-guidance-framework/controlling-or-coercive-behaviour-statutory-guidance-framework-accessible) lists a wide range of possible behaviors, including economic abuse, technology-facilitated abuse, false information, cyberstalking, hacking, doxxing, fake accounts, smart-device control, and threats or use of legal and administrative processes. The list is illustrative, not a finding that any listed event is abusive without context and evidence.

In the United States, [18 U.S.C. § 1589](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section1589) defines forced labor to include force or restraint, serious harm or threats of serious harm, abuse or threatened abuse of law or legal process, and schemes intended to make a person believe serious harm or restraint will follow if labor or services are withheld. This statute is narrower than coercive control generally: labor or services and the statutory elements must be proved.

### Violent online networks

The FBI’s March 2025 public-safety announcement describes violent online networks that target minors and other vulnerable people, build trust or romantic connections, then use manipulation, blackmail, threats, doxxing, swatting, and demands for escalating self-harm or sexual material. It identifies “764” among these networks while noting individualized motives. This is an official threat assessment, not a finding that a person reporting online coercion was targeted by that network. [FBI public-safety announcement](https://www.fbi.gov/investigate/cyber/alerts/2025/violent-online-networks-target-vulnerable-and-underage-populations-across-the-united-states-and-around-the-globe)

### Trafficking for forced criminality

UNODC’s 2025 paper documents trafficking into online scam centres and coercion through violence, debt, document control, confinement, and threats. It reports that INTERPOL had identified victims from 66 countries trafficked into scam centres by March 2025. The finding establishes a transnational forced-criminality problem; it should not be projected onto unrelated relationship or stalking reports. [UNODC working-group paper](https://www.unodc.org/documents/treaties/WG_TIP_2025/2/CTOC_COP_WG.4_2025_2_E.pdf)

### Technology-facilitated abuse

Australia’s eSafety Commissioner recommends considering device safety before acting, keeping evidence, and safety planning around technology changes. Research covers children and young people affected by technology-facilitated abuse in domestic and family violence. [eSafety help for people experiencing technology abuse](https://www.esafety.gov.au/key-topics/tech-based-abuse/i-may-be-experiencing-it), [eSafety research on children and technology-facilitated abuse](https://www.esafety.gov.au/research/children-and-technology-facilitated-abuse-in-domestic-and-family-violence-situations)

The Council of Europe uses “technology-facilitated violence against women and girls” for conduct including cyberstalking, harassment, privacy violations, threats, image abuse, and digitally extended domestic surveillance and control. [Council of Europe project overview](https://www.coe.int/en/web/cdpc/technology-facilitated-violence-against-women-and-girls)

## Institutional-failure and “not believed” findings

The strongest primary-source examples in this review are specific, not generalized:

- **F v M:** the family-court judgment identified the need to examine cumulative control and criticized aspects of the way a narrative had been accepted. What was later established was the court’s own fact finding on the civil standard—not every allegation made by either party.
- **Nassar / FBI:** DOJ OIG found serious failures, inaccurate or incomplete records, and false statements. This verifies failures in the defined investigation.
- **Rotherham:** the independent report documented years of exploitation and institutional failure to act on or properly understand victim evidence. The inquiry’s estimate is bounded to Rotherham and 1997–2013.
- **Post Office Horizon:** the public-inquiry record demonstrates large-scale harm where flawed technical evidence and institutional decisions overrode repeated contrary accounts. The causal system is materially different from domestic abuse.
- **Kate Wilson:** official admissions and tribunal findings prove that an officer used a false intimate identity and that protected rights were violated. The record supports scrutiny where objective evidence identifies official actors; it does not support assuming official involvement from thematic resemblance.
- **Volodina:** the ECHR found that repeated cyberviolence was not effectively investigated. It establishes a failure of state protection in that case.

These examples justify preserving reports, request logs, contemporaneous records, and evidence of how an institution responded. They do not justify treating every non-response, adverse decision, or disagreement as coordinated corruption.

## Cross-case linkage standard

The public site should never create a searchable accusation database or show named private individuals. That would risk defamation, retaliation, misidentification, and contamination of otherwise independent accounts.

A credible private linkage review should require:

1. reports developed independently before any proposed match is disclosed;
2. exact, objective identifiers such as a phone number, account handle, wallet address, payment destination, domain, company record, device identifier, file hash, uncommon document template, or verifiable organization;
3. documentation showing where each identifier came from and who handled it;
4. an estimate of how common the identifier is;
5. a written false-match and alternative-explanation analysis;
6. separation between an identifier match and an inference about who controlled it;
7. lawful handling, minimization, and access controls.

Shared emotions, broad tactics, demographic similarity, common phrases, or the fact that two people felt disbelieved are not adequate linkage evidence by themselves.

## Vulnerability and neurodivergence

### What is supported

- Australia’s Disability Royal Commission documents violence, abuse, neglect, and exploitation across settings and analyzes social and structural risk factors. [Final Report, Volume 3](https://disability.royalcommission.gov.au/publications/final-report-volume-3-nature-and-extent-violence-abuse-neglect-and-exploitation)
- The latest ONS victim-characteristics release reports higher measured domestic-abuse prevalence among disabled adults than non-disabled adults in England and Wales, with methodological cautions in the release. [ONS domestic-abuse victim characteristics](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/crimeandjustice/articles/domesticabusevictimcharacteristicsenglandandwales/latest)
- U.S. Bureau of Justice Statistics tables for 2009–2019 reported particularly high measured violent-victimization rates among people with cognitive disabilities. These are historical period estimates, not a current autism-specific rate. [BJS statistical tables](https://bjs.ojp.gov/library/publications/crime-against-persons-disabilities-2009-2019-statistical-tables)

### What remains unknown

The reviewed evidence does not establish a single organized program selecting autistic, ADHD, adopted, immigrant, socially isolated, or otherwise vulnerable people. An ethical study would need a defined population, comparison group, validated measures, recruitment-bias analysis, safeguarding, and a preregistered plan for distinguishing exposure, reporting, dependency, accessibility barriers, and deliberate targeting.

## Life-insurance and identity findings

- There is no universal public database showing every life-insurance policy on a living person.
- The [NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator](https://eapps.naic.org/life-policy-locator/) is for beneficiaries or authorized representatives searching for a deceased person’s policy or annuity. NAIC’s [September 2026 locator update](https://content.naic.org/article/naic-life-insurance-policy-locator-tool-helps-match-consumers-more-16-billion-lost-and-unclaimed) says searches may take 90 business days or more.
- An [MIB Consumer File](https://www.mib.com/request_your_record.html) may exist if a person applied for individually underwritten life or health insurance with an MIB member company within the prior seven years and underwriting-significant information was found. It can include coded information and recent inquiries; it is not a complete policy registry.
- [NAIC consumer guidance](https://content.naic.org/consumer/life-insurance.htm) explains that a purchaser needs an insurable interest to buy life insurance on another person; exact state law and remedies vary.
- A [state insurance department](https://content.naic.org/state-insurance-departments) is the appropriate regulator for suspected forged applications, agent or insurer misconduct, or insurance fraud questions.
- [IdentityTheft.gov](https://www.identitytheft.gov/) provides a tailored U.S. identity-theft recovery plan. The CFPB notes that a credit freeze does not block every insurance-purpose inquiry and does not prevent takeover of an existing account. [CFPB credit-freeze guidance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-credit-freeze-or-security-freeze-on-my-credit-report-en-1341/)

## Reporting principle

Route the **specific conduct** and keep a larger interpretation separate:

- imminent danger → local emergency services;
- stalking, assault, threats, or domestic abuse → local police and a specialist victim service;
- internet-enabled crime → the country’s cybercrime route, including [IC3](https://www.ic3.gov/) in the United States;
- identity theft → the relevant financial institution, credit bureau, insurer, regulator, and national identity-fraud route;
- trafficking or forced labor → a trafficking hotline or specialist service and law enforcement when safe;
- concrete DOJ or FBI employee misconduct → [DOJ OIG](https://oig.justice.gov/hotline/submit_complaint), after checking its scope; state and local police complaints generally belong elsewhere.

The first sentence of a report should be testable: “This account logged in from this address at this time,” “this message contains this threat,” or “this document contains a signature I did not make.” Attach the broader theory only after the events, sources, and requested action are clear.

## Public-site recommendations implemented

1. Keep the core phrase “evidence before explanation.”
2. Validate documented events without validating an unproved explanation.
3. Replace percentage-based self-scores with simple checklist counts and explicit non-diagnostic limits.
4. Keep pattern alignment, evidence readiness, and cross-case linkage separate.
5. Add a local-only incident worksheet that does not transmit, save, or autosave entries.
6. Warn visitors to use a safer device and not remove tracking technology without a safety plan.
7. Add exact “what this establishes / what this does not establish” limits to every research card.
8. Correct life-insurance copy so the NAIC locator is not presented as a way to find policies on a living person.
9. Add official and specialist routes for the United States, Canada, United Kingdom, Ireland, EU, Australia, New Zealand, India, South Africa, and global directories.
10. Keep alleged perpetrator names private and reject public accusation matching.
11. Treat vulnerability and neurodivergence as a bounded research question with selection-bias safeguards.
12. State plainly that no evidence reviewed establishes one worldwide organization behind all similar cases.

- Develop, with legal and security specialists, a threat model before considering any anonymous reporting system. Never promise “complete security” or absolute anonymity.
