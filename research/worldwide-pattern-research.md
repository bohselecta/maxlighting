# MAXLIGHTING Worldwide Pattern Research Brief

## Goal

Identify documented international cases, court decisions, government reports, peer-reviewed research, NGO/UN findings, and high-quality investigative reporting that resemble one or more MAXLIGHTING pattern categories: coercive control, persistent deception or fabricated narratives, technology-facilitated abuse, stalking/surveillance, malicious allegations, identity or financial exploitation, exploitation of vulnerable people, trafficking-related coercion, organized online coercion, corruption-enabled abuse, or retaliation that isolates or discredits a victim.

The research must **not assume that all matching cases belong to one organization or one crime**. The purpose is to find recurring mechanisms, jurisdictions, institutional responses, and objective overlaps that merit further investigation.

## Research questions

1. Which countries have court cases or official reports describing long-term coercive control in which the perpetrator maintained a consistent false narrative despite contradictory evidence?
2. Where have investigators documented fabricated records, false accusations, staged evidence, malicious prosecution, or deliberate credibility attacks as part of abuse, trafficking, organized crime, cultic control, or fraud?
3. Which jurisdictions have strong documentation of technology-facilitated abuse: spyware, account compromise, impersonation, tracking, doxxing, swatting, non-consensual imagery, platform harassment, or coordinated online coercion?
4. Where do identity theft, synthetic identity fraud, forced or coerced account use, sham relationships/marriages, benefit fraud, insurance fraud, financial exploitation, or document fraud intersect with coercion or trafficking?
5. Are neurodivergent, disabled, socially isolated, adopted/fostered, immigrant, financially vulnerable, or otherwise marginalized victims described disproportionately in credible source material? Distinguish measured prevalence from anecdote and account for selection bias.
6. Which countries have official cases involving public officials, police, intelligence, military, immigration, court, prison, or other state actors participating in, enabling, ignoring, or corruptly protecting abuse or trafficking? Treat corruption as established only where credible evidence supports it.
7. Which cases include survivors initially being dismissed as unstable, unbelievable, paranoid, or mentally ill before later evidence corroborated material parts of their reports?
8. Are there recurring objective identifiers across otherwise unrelated cases: the same companies, financial intermediaries, online platforms, document types, recruitment methods, coercive phrases, institutional pathways, insurance mechanisms, or criminal services?
9. Which legal systems have the clearest terminology and remedies for coercive control, psychological abuse, technology-facilitated abuse, trafficking coercion, stalking, organized harassment, identity exploitation, and malicious use of legal or administrative systems?
10. What victim-support, reporting, and evidence-preservation resources exist in each jurisdiction?

## Geographic scope

Search globally, not only English-language countries. Prioritize, but do not limit research to:

- United States and Canada
- United Kingdom and Ireland
- European Union and EEA countries
- Ukraine, Russia, the Balkans, Caucasus, and Eastern Europe
- Israel and the broader Middle East
- India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, and Sri Lanka
- Southeast Asia
- Japan and South Korea
- Australia and New Zealand
- Mexico, Central America, the Caribbean, and South America
- Sub-Saharan Africa and North Africa

Use native-language searching or translated queries when it materially improves coverage.

## Source hierarchy

Rank evidence in this order:

**Tier 1 — Primary / official**
- court opinions and filed judgments
- indictments, plea agreements, sentencing records, and official case files
- police/prosecutor/inspector-general/ombudsman reports
- parliamentary or congressional inquiries
- national trafficking, cybercrime, anti-corruption, domestic-abuse, and victim-service reports
- UN, UNODC, IOM, Council of Europe, Europol, Interpol, OSCE, EU Agency reports

**Tier 2 — High-quality research**
- peer-reviewed journals
- university research centers
- systematic reviews and meta-analyses
- reputable legal scholarship

**Tier 3 — Investigative reporting / NGO documentation**
- major investigative news organizations with named sourcing and records
- established anti-trafficking, domestic-violence, stalking, cyber-abuse, and human-rights NGOs

**Tier 4 — Lead only**
- forums, social media, personal blogs, anonymous claims, unverified survivor narratives

Tier 4 material may generate search leads but must never be presented as verified evidence without independent corroboration.

## Required classification for every finding

For every candidate case, label each claim as one of:

- **VERIFIED / DOCUMENTED** — directly established by a primary record or strong official source.
- **RESEARCH-SUPPORTED PATTERN** — supported by credible research but not necessarily proven in this individual case.
- **SURVIVOR / WITNESS ALLEGATION** — reported by a person but not independently established.
- **INFERENCE / HYPOTHESIS** — a possible connection proposed by the researcher.
- **UNKNOWN / CONTRADICTED** — unsupported, disputed, or contradicted by stronger evidence.

Never collapse these categories.

## Pattern coding

For each case, score presence/absence/uncertain for:

- persistent narrative commitment / unusually consistent false framing
- gaslighting / reality manipulation
- isolation or dependency
- threats or intimidation
- physical violence
- sexual coercion
- financial coercion or theft
- identity/document fraud
- insurance or benefit exploitation
- sham marriage / immigration exploitation
- trafficking or forced labor
- technology surveillance / account compromise
- impersonation / synthetic identity use
- malicious allegations or fabricated records
- weaponization of police/courts/medical systems
- reputational destruction / employment sabotage
- coercion by group, family, cult, gang, extremist network, employer, or criminal organization
- corruption or misconduct by public officials
- victim initially dismissed as unstable/unreliable
- later objective corroboration
- neurodivergence/disability/vulnerability noted in record
- cross-border element
- online recruitment or trust-building
- blackmail/extortion/doxxing/swatting
- death, near-death, imprisonment, hospitalization, or disappearance as an outcome

## Deliverable format

Produce:

### 1. Executive map
A country-by-country table with:
- country
- number of high-confidence matching cases found
- strongest documented pattern categories
- relevant statutes or official terminology
- best official reporting/victim-support contacts
- confidence level

### 2. Case matrix
For each case:
- case name / docket / year
- jurisdiction
- source links
- actors and relationship type
- concise chronology
- exact documented mechanisms
- evidence type
- outcome
- pattern codes
- what is similar to MAXLIGHTING
- what is materially different
- whether any cross-case identifiers appear

### 3. Strongest pattern matches
Select the 10–25 cases worldwide with the most unusually specific overlap. Explain why each is notable without claiming common coordination unless evidence supports it.

### 4. Institutional-failure cases
Separate section for cases in which police, courts, hospitals, immigration systems, prisons, regulators, military/intelligence personnel, or other public institutions demonstrably failed, enabled abuse, retaliated, or were corruptly involved.

### 5. Victim-not-believed cases
Collect well-documented examples where material allegations were initially dismissed as implausible, unstable, paranoid, fabricated, or mentally ill and were later corroborated in significant part. Be precise about what was and was not later proven.

### 6. International terminology index
For each country/language, identify the local legal/research terms closest to:
- coercive control
- gaslighting / psychological manipulation
- technology-facilitated abuse
- stalking
- trafficking coercion
- identity exploitation
- malicious prosecution / false reporting / fabricated evidence
- organized online coercion

### 7. MAXLIGHTING update recommendations
Recommend additions to the public site only when supported by strong evidence. Include localized resource links and note where a claim should remain a research question rather than public factual copy.

## Analytical safeguards

- Do not assume a single global syndicate.
- Do not infer nationality, religion, political ideology, intelligence affiliation, or extremist affiliation from style, language, appearance, or rumor.
- Do not treat similarity as proof of coordination.
- Do not use unsupported accusations against named private individuals.
- Prefer boring documentary links—court records, money flows, account records, corporate links, phone numbers, repeated intermediaries, shared infrastructure—over thematic resemblance.
- Actively search for disconfirming evidence and alternative explanations.
- Note source-access limitations and translation uncertainty.
- Preserve contradictory evidence rather than smoothing it away.

## Final research standard

The project succeeds if it produces a defensible international evidence base showing **which mechanisms are genuinely documented, where they occur, how authorities describe them, how victims are affected, and where unusually specific overlaps deserve further investigation**.

It does not succeed by proving a predetermined theory.
